# Pattern 2: Colon + three-item list → natural sentence

Read this rule, fix the letter, score the applied level, record
evidence. One rule at a time.

## Rule

A colon is fine when genuinely useful, but avoid using it to
introduce a predictable three-part list.

## Before → After

Before:
"I learned how the platform works: what it does, who uses it, and how data moves through it."

After:
"I spent the first few weeks getting to know the platform, its users, and how data moves through it."

## Rubric

Quality: references/quality-rubric.md, minimum acceptable.
Coverage: references/completeness-rubric.md, minimum all.

## Coverage

Sweep every paragraph and every colon. A colon is fine when
genuinely useful; the violation is a colon introducing a
predictable three-part list.

## Ask

- Could I predict this list before reading the sentence?

## Do not

- Do not automatically structure sentences as: "X: A, B, and C."
